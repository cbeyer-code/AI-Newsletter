## Highlights of the Week

There is a wonderful and funny interview by Jon Stewart with Jacob Coxon, the ex-OpenAI and Anthropic engineer, who retired for fear of future model capabilities towards the human race.  
He explains that recursive self-improvement is very close, that models currently being trained will be even more powerful, and they discuss implications for our society, including jobs and defining human meaning.   
These topics are discussed more extensively in this New York Times article (What Would You Do If Your Employer Could Destroy the World?) with a lot of former frontier Lab employees.  
<https://archive.is/20261005163117/https://nymag.com/intelligencer/article/ai-researchers-quit-openai-anthropic.html>  
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lnLDjXy2kRA>

The second highlight is that OpenAI released over 700 new proofs for outstanding mathematical problems, including partial solutions to the Riemann Hypothesis.  
It is being claimed that multiple of these proofs would warrant a Fields medal, the highest honor in mathematics.  
<https://openai.com/index/sharing-ai-progress-in-mathematics/>  
Stunningly, this was achieved by the model they plan to release next and only required 3 hours of Pro use on average, which is completely insane. There are PhDs and Professors unsuccessfully grinding away on these problems for decades, and GPT-7 does it in 3 hours.  
The caveat is that OpenAI tried to solve many more problems, mostly unsuccessfully, as they only got (partial) solutions for 5%.   
Furthermore, Scott Aaronson quotes his wife, who worked for years on one of the now-solved problems: ‘It feels like something written by someone who’s on psychedelics. So much unclear and doesn’t make sense…**The UGC proof invents a completely new bizarre code with a noise test. It’s some crazy recursive construction. It’s not the long code, not the short code – some alien craziness.**’  
The blog post is highly exciting and also points out other problems, for instance, how to communicate these results. OpenAI threw them all out, whereas Anthropic was now cooperating with selected mathematicians to turn results into humanly accessible papers, which makes Anthropic the kingmakers, deciding who gets to publish these huge findings.  
Which one is worse? **Furthermore, he points out that cryptography is suspiciously absent from the pile of proofs, and people fear this might mean that future models can break established data encryption protocols.**  
<https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10169>  
Large parts of the math community are up in arms, and some are even calling for stopping cooperation with frontier labs, including the German math association, which suggests hiding future math research behind a paywall so AI cannot readily access it.  
<https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/07/ahm-statement-on-openais-october-6-release-of-mathematical-documents/>  
<https://www.mathematik.de/dmv-presse/5354-kapern-einzelne-technologie-konzerne-mittels-k%C3%BCnstlicher-intelligenz-die-mathematik>  
Scot Aaronson also discusses here whether these are really novel contributions or combinations of existing solution approaches that humans missed, which is mostly true, but he also points out that the critics are continuously moving the goalposts, while AI is consistently scoring goals they previously set as the benchmark. The goalposts are so far out that only contributions on Einstein-level seem to count, which cannot be achieved by 99.99999% of humans either.   
Also, it seems that some of the proofs could contain really novel approaches.  
‘**The "aha" insight for this is actually f\*\*ing wild, it involves a complex valued exponential sum on the edges.** **I've seen a lot of clever counting arguments before in graph theory but this is the first time I've seen complex roots and annihilating terms like this, the symbolic manipulation tricks in this look like things out of quantum physics. I don't understand where this trick originated, I need to really digest this.**’  
<https://x.com/josusanmartin/status/2107690318107689004>  
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6bpaH3TIf7Y>  
  
For me, it is also always a pleasure to see AI doubters eventually come around and acknowledge the speed of development and growing capabilities. Just have a look at the websites Opus 5.5 created for this software developer.  
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dyEpDobnkmA>

## Models 

This week, some true European open-weight models came out that seem to be interesting.  
First of all, Mistral 4 large (Le Chonk), which is a 1T-parameter model with 49B active, is natively multimodal and has 1M tokens of context. It seems to outperform frontier models on visual grounding and is the best open-weights model from the USA and Europe when it comes to aggregated benchmarks. Still, it only scores 38 on the artificial intelligence index, only 4 points ahead of QWEN 3.8 27B, a much smaller model.  
<https://x.com/MistralAI/status/2107457414387622310>  
<https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4/>  
<https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/mistral-large-4>  
Open-weights come in 3 weeks:  
<https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Large-4-1T-A52B>  
  
The other is Kolibri from Germany, which is a 78B model with 3.46B active parameters, and up to 1M tokens of context. It is specialized in German and English, and the developers claim it to be on the Pareto frontier of similarly sized models.   
It might be useful if you have to work a lot with German text or want to generate German output, but otherwise I don’t see why you would want to use it and not QWEN 3.8 27B, for instance, or a Flash version of other powerful Chinese models.  
<https://x.com/Aleph__Alpha/status/2106306840657297814>  
<https://aleph-alpha.com/en/blog/kolibri-has-landed-a-sovereign-open-weight-model/>  
<https://huggingface.co/Aleph-Alpha/Kolibri-1>

Reflection released a new American open-weight model called Beam. ‘Beam is a sparse Mixture-of-Experts model with 501 billion total parameters, 23 billion active, built for coding, reasoning, and agentic workloads.’ They claim it is more token-efficient than Chinese models, but we will only know once the weights are released and people try it.  
<https://x.com/reflection_ai/status/2107186849370247235>  
<https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam>

LuiquidAI released two Jev-like, open, multimodal decision models, which rank first among models under 10B.  
<https://x.com/liquidai/status/2107878924831379676>  
<https://www.liquid.ai/blog/d1-open>  
Here you can see it playing Snake, making one decision every 8ms.  
<https://x.com/atomic_chat_hq/status/2107931155840299417>  
  
Google released Gemma Embedding 2, which can turn text, images, audio, and video into vectors that can be compared by similarity, so you could build applications where you could look for the song closest to a text or video snippet, which should enable many cool ideas.  
<https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2107502286758895878>
