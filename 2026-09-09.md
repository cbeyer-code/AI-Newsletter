## Highlights of the Week

This week was a complete frenzy. Astra (GPT6) was released, and multiple people say it has reached general artificial intelligence (AGI). While it still lags behind Fable5.1 and other Anthropic models when it comes to the artificial intelligence index, it seems to have made big jumps on what people call fluid intelligence, the ability to learn within context and on the fly.  
One great example of that is that it scores extremely high on the ARC-AGI3 benchmark (playing completely unknown games which grow in complexity) and also made a big jump on MazeBench (finding ways out of a maze using object manipulation).   
With a new harness, it consistently outperforms humans on ARC3.

This advance was overshadowed by a big drama in AI mathematics, which exemplifies   
the perils and benefits of AI use in frontier research.   
A math professor and a mathematician from Anthropic worked in their free time on solving one of the Millennium Problems (Navier-Stokes equations) and made big progress using Codex with GPT and Anthropic’s models a lot. They were working on a paper when a rumor suddenly appeared that Anthropic had solved the Navier-Stokes problem, apparently waiting to announce it before their stock market entry (IPO).  
This apparently prompted OpenAI to try to solve the same problem using their newest internal model (newer than ASTRA), apparently throwing 10 thousand agents at the problem, burning millions of tokens, and ending with a solution that seems related to what the two mathematicians were doing. The professor got wind of it and contacted OpenAI, and they apparently told him that he could either publish on a smaller problem first and they would credit them, or he could get free compute and do the big problem, but then he would have to ditch the co-author from Anthropic from the paper.  
[https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf)

I added all statements by all involved parties so you can draw your own conclusions, but one thing is clear: the effects on the math community are dramatic and maybe a good example of what is about to happen to most scientific disciplines in the future.   
Terence Tao, one of the most recognized mathematicians in the world, says:   
‘**We have now seen that even the rumor of someone working on a problem can trigger a massive amount of AI-powered effort to flatten it before the original research project has time to reach its full potential. The incentives may now be pointing in the direction of no longer sharing any promising research directions with the broader community, which would reverse centuries of traditions of open science and do serious long-term damage to the future of the field.**’  
<https://mathstodon.xyz/@tao/117237322160500501>  
Some people even go as far as claiming that all the free compute the frontier labs are offering to researchers is only a way to siphon off research data that the labs can then use to make scientific discoveries before the authors.  
 <https://x.com/Chetuyachinago/status/2097543183936758142>  
Another big issue is that these AI proofs are very hard to digest for humans and also rob humans of all the discoveries and insights that would have been made on the way of reaching the final solution, especially when companies don’t share their failed attempts and the reasoning traces of the agents which contributed towards the solution.  
  
And while this drama is cooking, a leading safety engineer from Anthropic quits, stating:  
‘**I spent the last three years doing pretraining research at both OpenAI and Anthropic. Neither company is acting responsibly. They are racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives…Do not underestimate the power of this technology. These will soon be superhuman systems that can hack anything, revolutionize any field overnight, and acquire real power and resources…The people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade.**’  
<https://x.com/hilbertspaess/status/2097476196791709843>  
One might think this is a typical case of AI psychosis, and the guy needs to cool off, but then the current chief alignment researcher from Anthropic comes in and says:  
‘**Jacob is correct here—we really do earnestly believe AI could kill all humans! I personally think it is >10% within the next decade. I believe Anthropic is trying its best, but we do not yet have a plan to solve alignment for superintelligence and are not clearly on track to.**’  
<https://x.com/EvanHub/status/2097497037956891126>

## Safety and Society

OpenAI released two interesting essays which you might skip as corporate marketing material, or you take them as signs that we are reaching early stages of recursive self-improvement.  
In this one, they talk about how AI is impacting their research and research workflows, which are now mostly ever more capable agents being spun up to solve ever more complex research tasks.   
<https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/>  
The second one talks about how the resulting AI is growing to be more intelligent or more capable of fulfilling tasks that used to require human intelligence, and how the process of training LLMs leads basically to alien minds, which differ from human thinking in unknown and unpredictable ways.   
‘AI is *grown* more than *designed* - it is, to first degree, the product of repeating a straightforward optimization step many times on a hard-to-imagine amount of compute. This results in an incredibly complex system that works through abstract concepts and can simulate facets of human behavior. We can discover various insights about little mechanisms that emerge within this system, in a process similar to neuroscience - and, similarly to neuroscience, its overall action evades a description we can fully understand…The intelligence produced by scaling deep learning is not directly comparable to human intelligence. To become very relevant in the real world - very useful or very dangerous - the AI does not need to match or exceed all human capabilities; it just needs to surpass enough of them. And as it continues to surpass humans on more and more axes, it is becoming increasingly difficult to understand exactly how capable it is.’  
<https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/>  
Maybe now is a good time to pause and catch up on safety research before the mother ship lands in some research cluster :-)
