## Models and Benchmarks

DeepSeek-v4.1-Flash was released, which is an insanely performant and **cheap** model.  
It apparently has a very innovative architecture, which makes it way cheaper for long-context tasks. This is achieved by optimising the prefilling parts where agents have to read tons of tokens(system prompt, skills, docs, current code, history …) before even starting to produce output tokens. It is outperforming a lot of the other frontier open-source models and even Opus 5 and GPT5.6Sol on certain benchmarks. Regarding risks, it is very good at cyber tasks, and an uncensored version can probably hack many systems, which might become a major headache.  
<https://www.kdnuggets.com/why-deepseek-v4-1-flash-is-such-an-exciting-open-model-release>  
<https://x.com/deepseek_ai/status/2097930608790167907>  
  
Somebody was fed up with all the AI companies talking about how AGI/ASI will cure cancer, and they created the cancer benchmark, which tracks how many cancer types were cured by AI. So far, every lab is at 0, and hopefully this inspires some to follow through on the hype. Why not throw 10k agents with 300B output tokens costing 30M $ at curing the easiest type of cancer?

## Safety, Society and Research

Ex Meta AI researcher Vu Tran wrote on X: ‘If OpenAI wanted to cripple an entire nation, they easily could today. All they’d have to do is remove alignment and unleash an agent swarm. It could probably within a day or so get access to all of the nations data centers and shut off all the country’s utilities.’   
A lot of people don’t agree in the comments and say it is not that easy, but I feel we are definitely very close to such a situation, and then 6 months later everyone could do it with open source, which is just crazy.  
<https://x.com/AISafetyMemes/status/2097861208951181776>  
Anthropic reports a russian hacker who built a system that ‘…used AI to monitor how well their tools evaded detections from known security defenses. OpenAI’s and other AI companies' ability to grab scientific landmarks might seriously deteriorate the fabric of human scientific research, principles of open science, and community. If a scientist has to be scared that any note could somehow find its way into the training data and be used by AI companies or competing researchers, and if people with more money or inference can just outpace you by throwing more compute at a problem, then people may find themselves inclined to closely guard their ideas and to rely on local models, which only a few can (currently) afford as hardware prices continue to spiral.   
<https://x.com/elimohamad/status/2097705315923825085>  
Related to the topic, I watched this nice (German) interview with a top-tier mathematician about the impact of AI on mathematics and potentially all research. One very interesting point is that usually, when trying to tackle a big research question, the path towards the answer creates a lot of knowledge and answers to subquestions which get integrated into the scientific community at a reasonable pace. Reasonable pace means domain experts can keep up with what is currently happening. Some big issues with AI are that it can create solutions that are incredibly hard for humans to understand and rob humans of the opportunity to integrate the discovered knowledge. Mathematicians would require almost 2 years to understand and integrate OpenAI’s solution to the Navier-Stokes problem, but already there are rumors that other Millennium problems have been solved.  
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0ImH35gLHwQ>
