**Continual learning via sleeping**  
Google has published new research regarding continuous learning, which would enable AI to constantly learn new things and adjust without having to be retrained from scratch.   
Currently, models have a knowledge cut-off, and they don’t know what happened after that date because they are state-less machines that require in-context learning or context injection in order to adapt to new information or scenarios. Online adaptation of LLMs often leads to catastrophic forgetting, where an AI gets worse at skills it was good at. But now   
Google has developed a technique that is inspired by human sleep, which claims that:  
*‘…alternating between active interaction and offline memory consolidation allows LLMs to continuously acquire new facts, retain long-context knowledge up to 10M tokens, and improve complex reasoning.’*  
If this turns out to be practical, it would be huge.  
<https://research.google/blog/the-sleep-framework-a-new-way-for-llms-to-retain-knowledge-in-continual-learning/>

**LLMs reading and tuning weights directly**  
A new paper suggests that newer frontier models managed to directly manipulate the weights of older and smaller open-weight LLMs, reducing their tendency to loop.  
<https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/>  
The work was replicated by multiple people, for example, here:  
<https://x.com/AlexKrentsel/status/2117376683724660898>  
And here somebody showed that the LLM could sometimes   
guess from the weights what another LLM was doing:  
<https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/3/qwen-can-interpret-model-weights/>  
Other people are calling it bullshit and overhyped, so it remains to be seen how relevant this will be, but in the worst case, AI could surgically remove alignment or capability blockers that might have been baked into an LLM for safety.  
<https://x.com/dummerspast39/status/2117416640132366570>

**More bang for less buck**  
A new paper suggests that agentic coding wastes a ton of tokens on interpreting tool output in order to decide the next step, but often has already planned the next step in advance.   
If a simple quality check passes, then we can skip the costly reasoning and go straight to the pre-decided next step. On Codex, this kept quality the same but saved around 30% tokens, whereas DeepSeek harness saved almost 90% and even improved output quality.  
I hope they add it to the next release of the harness.  
<https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2117177813652709633>  
<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32813>  
  
**Compaction doesn’t hurt**  
A new paper from NVIDIA suggests that context compaction did not deteriorate model performance as much as expected and actually can lead to a speed-up and a reduction in costs. If this is true, it would be really great for local AI and inference in general as it would allow to run models with smaller context windows in low VRAM settings.  
<https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2117956281910829083>

**Robots secretly share secrets**  
Two frontier LLMs, each running a robot and being able to see the other LLM’s robot, managed to exchange secrets without discussing a messaging protocol in advance, which is possible due to their similar stochastic ‘brains’. Basically, guessing what the other LLM is doing is so easy because the LLM can just ask itself,   
‘*What would I be doing if I were to behave like this?’*  
<https://x.com/robocurve/status/2116704239003349503>
