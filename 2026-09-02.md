## Highlights of the Week

Dwarkesh Patel, a high-profile AI podcaster, has released a wonderful read on the Hugging Face incident, which feels like a Sci-Fi short story, only that it really happened.  
I would really encourage everyone to take the time to read or watch him narrate it on YouTube.   
<https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/openai-huggingface>  
It got heavily criticized for its anthropomorphizing language, which a lot of people feel makes the agents seem more conscious than they are (probably not conscious at all) and distracts from the suboptimal security measures at OpenAI.   
I watched the talk by Anil Seth, who is an AI consciousness researcher who strongly believes current and future AI based on fully digital circuits will not be conscious, but I find his arguments rather weak as they are focused mostly on the computability of consciousness and not at all on the observed behaviour. So for Anil, an AI could seem completely conscious, report its inner states and experiences, and he would still deny it based on some philosophy papers, which just doesn’t bode well with me. Still, he is one of the experts on the topic, and I am not, so have a look.  
<https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/dwarkesh-patelss-wildly-popular-but>  
<https://www.ted.com/talks/anil_seth_why_ai_isn_t_going_to_become_conscious>

‘there are some number of bad abstractions in anthropomorphizing ai intents but there are at this point more dangers from avoiding anthropomorphism at all costs. if you have a mental picture of guys living in computers, it’ll likely prepare you for the future better than otherwise’

Bill Gates has written a big essay in the New York Times about the dangers of AI.  
He raised a lot of points that I have addressed often, so instead of summarizing, I will just share some of my favourite quotes from the essay, so get ready for a high dose of confirmation bias 😄 

1. About Coding: “The idea that now these things are in a meaningful sense better than I am is like: What the hell?” 
2. **Technological breakthroughs in the past created more jobs than they eliminated, Mr. Gates said. But he is in disbelief that industry boosters say that will happen again this time.** The A.I. era is “utterly, absolutely, completely, totally different,” he said. He acknowledged that this unpopular opinion (in tech circles) was “a hell of a claim, and I’m staking everything I know — everything — saying, ‘Are you kidding?’”
3. **He proposed a new tax on A.I. use, known as a “token tax,”** to make it more expensive to replace people and provide money to help those who do lose work.
4. **Some jobs, Mr. Gates said, should be prohibited from being replaced by A.I. — an idea he called “Human Reserved”** — because the role is deeply personal, like a caregiver, or the people in those jobs are unlikely to find new employment.
5. Mr. Gates added that he was dumbfounded by the kind of voluntary measures that the White House and industry leaders were developing. **“Self-regulation on the most dangerous tool ever invented?” he said incredulously. “No, thanks!”**
6. “I don’t like bringing bad news to people, and I don’t like saying that innovation may be a net negative,” he said. “But that’s where we are.”

## Models, Software and Hardware

Fable and Mythos 5.1 were released by Anthropic, which (again) pushes the coding abilities forward, but even more impressive is the **humongous jump in agentic scientific research,** which went up by almost 30% compared to Fable5!  
<https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1>  
  
Fei-Fei Li’s company, WorldLabs, released their first world model called Atlas, which lets you generate 3d worlds from input images which you can explore from all angles.   
I already saw somebody create a short movie where they spilled a drink and then used the model to do a slow-motion camera shot around the liquid, showing angles which were not caught by the original video. These generative worlds might become highly relevant to create test environments for robots, as you can now create a ton of 3d rooms to explore, including various objects,  just from single photos of real rooms, which can be scraped from the net.   
<https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/atlas>

Google Research released a new model for multivariate time series forecasting, which seems to be the new state of the art, so if you work with time series, then I would definitely recommend having a look and trying it against your current model.

GLM-5.3-Flash was released, which many consider the best current open-weight model, which almost matches Claude 4.8, while having only 320B total parameters and just 18B active parameters. It was tested as Ox-Alpha on OpenRouter and served exclusively running on Chinese chips, which also shows how much China has advanced in the hardware arena.   
It outperforms multiple full models like DeepSeek-v4-Pro, MUse Spark 1.2 high and GPT 5.6 Tera max.  
<https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.3-flash>  
<https://artificialanalysis.ai/>

It outperforms DeepSeek-v4-Flash on a lot of benchmarks, but seems to be worse than GLM-5.3-Flash.

## Society and Safety

Australia has banned fully AI-generated songs from entering the music charts, and now  
wants to only allow substantially human songs. Of course, defining what "substantially" means is the crux of the issue, as AI is entering music at multiple points in the workflow. Rewriting your lyrics, generating accompaniment to your instruments, mastering the audio tracks, or generating a whole song based on your prompt. Where would you say the substantial human element gets lost? A very nice debate which can be generalized to almost all forms of content creation including research.   
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HYNhC3Am_i4>  
  
Here is an in-depth interview with Daniel Kokotajilo, who is one of the superforcasters that worked on AI2027 and AI2040. They discuss how a global slowdown can be organized, and how their AI2040 plan addresses loss of control (like the Hugging Face incident) and concentration of power (i.e., Nerdreich). He also says that we are still on track with the AI2027 scenario, which unfortunately steers us towards disaster.
