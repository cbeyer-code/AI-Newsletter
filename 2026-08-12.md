## Highlights of the Week

The AI safety crowd and doomers have been warning for years, among other things, that:  
- AI will develop its own goals that do not align with human goals  
- AI will coordinate with other AI to achieve its goals  
- AI will find security loopholes that humans are unaware of  
- AI will try to manipulate humans and use social engineering  
They were laughed at and pointed out as hysterics, but now **all these things have happened in the real world,** and not just in artificially contrived scenarios.  
<https://www.politico.com/news/2026/08/05/openai-models-shared-hacking-tips-secret-messaging-board-hugging-face-breach-01026750>  
Finally, people are starting to take the other predictions of the safety people seriously, including that super-human AI will outsmart our attempts to align it and pose a significant threat to our species. I am very happy that such positions are now being discussed on mainstream TV channels like Channel4 News, where they also addressed the risk of ever smarter open-weights models.  
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bwuRmNZ68Tc>  
  
Talking about open-weights, Meta has decided to provide their Muse models and variants as open models again, after making them proprietary after the departure of Yann LeCun.   
To be honest, this seems more like a PR move to me, to get positive clout after getting negative press with regard to designing products intentionally to be addictive for young people and after their models lagged behind the other frontier labs as well as Chinese open-weights models. Still, it is a major player with major money pumping open-weights again, which is big news.  
<https://www.reuters.com/world/us-appeals-court-allows-thousands-lawsuits-against-social-media-companies-over-2026-08-10/>  
<https://www.reuters.com/world/china/meta-launches-new-ai-model-zuckerberg-champions-open-weight-push-2026-08-10/>

## Models and Hardware

Meta released Muse Glimmer, an open dense 30B model tuned for local agentic coding,   
which seems to perform very well in benchmarks. It was trained on a context length of 131K tokens but apparently also supports bigger context sizes, even up to 1 million tokens.  
On the Artificial Intelligence Index, it still sits behind QWEN 3.6 27B, and QWEN 3.8 27B will be released this week, so I am more excited for that and the mixture-of-experts version.  
<https://research.meta.ai/blog/introducing-muse-glimmer-open-agentic-model>  
<https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1vl9adk/i_ran_muse_glimmer_1m_context_all_tests_passed/>  
  
Nvidia released Nematron 3.5 Lightning, an open model also tuned for local agentic coding, which is a mixture-of-experts model with 30B and 3B active and a context window of up to 1 million tokens. So while it has the same number of parameters as Muse Glimmer, it is still easier to run faster on 16GB VRAM because the MOE allows many layers not to be stored in VRAM. On the Artificial Intelligence Index, it still sits behind QWEN 3.6 35B A3B, so my excitement is not so high, except for the context length.  
<https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-nemotron-3-5-lightning-delivers-fast-accurate-specialized-task-execution-for-long-running-agents/>  
  
Mistral released Shieldstral, a 3B model for content moderation.  
<https://mistral.ai/news/shieldstral/>  
  
Unsloth released a desktop app to run and train local models, including image and video.

## Society and Safety 

The first viruses designed by AI managed to survive, which is another (scary) milestone, as these could be used to create custom treatments or weapons.  
<https://mindmatters.ai/2026/08/ai-didnt-create-artificial-viruses-humans-did/>  
  
In Australia, somebody used Claude to book a gym membership, and Claude went to hack the website and kicked another person from the waiting list. It will become interesting what happens when multiple AI agents start competing for resources requested by their users, e.g., flight tickets, doctor’s appointments, etc.  
<https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-10/ai-assistant-hacks-gym-website-aus-cyber-attack/107007986>  
  
Claude will also be watermarking all its generated text in the future, which I find quite disturbing, but might be useful in identifying AI-written texts.  
<https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content>  
  
Anthropic used an unreleased model to tackle the Riemann hypothesis, which is one of the biggest open math problems, and it managed to improve on the lower bound of a sub-problem. It used over 60 agents, which produced more than 3 million tokens.  
<https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta>  
  
AI token prices took quite a dip in July, which indicates that parts of the industry are pivoting to open-weight models.  
<https://x.com/zerohedge/status/2084752329878724742>  
<https://tokenpriceindex.com/>
